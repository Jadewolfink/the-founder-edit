I used to think time was limited. So I rushed.

Everybody who knows me knows I walk fast. Really fast. I didn't always walk like this. But somewhere along the way, I stopped waiting. I walk, I move, and I'm already on to the next thing.

I notice people who walk slowly. I'm almost embarrassed to admit it's become one of my criteria when I date. If you walk slowly, it's not going to work. We'd be moving at different paces.

I rushed my breakfast. I rushed my coffee. I rushed to the next meeting, the next call, the next thing.

And before I knew it, it had been ten years of just rushing.

I became a productivity queen. I optimised everything. Even my trip to the toilet has a routine that saves five extra steps.

And still, I felt like I never had enough time. Honestly, I still feel that way.

## The word that stopped me

I'd started reading a little Chinese philosophy. And one day, the word for busy came to me. 忙. Máng.

I must have written it a thousand times without really looking at it. This time, I looked.

On the left, 忄: the heart. On the right, 亡: to lose, to perish.

Busy, written out, means *the heart, dying*.

I was amazed. Not only by what it said, but by the beauty of the language itself. One small character, holding a whole warning inside it.

I sat with that for a long time. Because it was true. All those years of optimising, and I'd been quietly killing my own heart.

I'd stopped tasting the cereal I ate in the morning. I didn't enjoy the latte I bought. I paid for it, I drank it, and I was already somewhere else.

## Just swallow it

Growing up, my mother would say: just swallow it.

So I did. For most of my life, I gulped things down. Food, drinks, days.

Nowadays, I don't just swallow it. I enjoy it.

That's the whole difference.

Even a cup of Milo. I really enjoy my Milo now, and that means more to me than it sounds. I'm grateful I get to go for sound healing, too, and actually be there for it.

It's in the little things. Taking the time to notice them, and appreciating them for what they are. It changes everything. My perspective, all of it.

## And I'm not the only one

We wear the rush like a badge. Retire at 30. Retire at 40. Finally free. So we sprint: the faster promotion, the next salary band, the earlier exit.

We save our joy for one week a year, the family holiday, when we'll finally slow down and enjoy it.

But life isn't the holiday. Life is the 51 other weeks too. The Tuesday breakfast. The coffee on the way to work. The cup of Milo.

So what are we rushing for?

If the journey is the life, I don't want to spend it racing past every flower on the way.

I still walk fast. But I don't rush my Milo anymore.

It turns out the time was never the thing running out. My heart was.
